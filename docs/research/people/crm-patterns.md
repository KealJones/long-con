# Personal CRM and organization patterns

Research for Long Con "people cards" and dated interest notes. Nothing here is built; it's the data models, the evidence on contact cadence, and storage patterns worth stealing. Every claim links to a page I actually read. Where a source is a competitor's blog or a press release, it's flagged.

## 1. What the tools actually store

### Personal CRMs

**Monica (open source).** The README lists: relationships between contacts, reminders (plus automatic birthday reminders), activities with a contact, "how you met," notes, addresses and contact methods, pets, labels, favorites, tasks, documents/photos, and a diary for your own life ([Monica README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monicahq/monica/main/README.md)). The marketing page adds gifts ("offered or that you want to offer"), debts/loans in both directions, call logging that feeds reminders, and important dates ([monicahq.com/features](https://monicahq.com/features)). The newer docs make everything a **module** inside a per-contact **template** of "pages" (tabs), and put contacts inside private **vaults**. Journal posts can link to a contact ([Monica docs](https://docs.monicahq.com/llms-full.txt)). The weakness is all manual: Monica "requires you to manually enter every interaction," and has no email, calendar or LinkedIn sync. Activities can carry an optional emotion tag. Per-contact reminders fire at 30 days, 7 days and the day itself ([Dex's Monica review, a competitor's blog](https://getdex.com/blog/monica-review/)).

**Clay, renamed Mesh in March 2026** ([Mesh announcement](https://me.sh/stories/mesh)). It builds contacts from email, calendar and social accounts, optionally adds iMessage (which needs "full disk access"), enriches entries from the web (LinkedIn background, recent tweets), and records how you met ([TechCrunch 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/30/clay-debuts-a-new-tool-to-help-people-better-manage-their-business-and-personal-relationships)). **Nexus** is a natural-language query layer ("who knows about chip manufacturing", "who's been to X"). It reads email *metadata only*, not bodies. It launched as a technical preview with a once-a-month data refresh, and Twitter data got worse after the API changes ([TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/16/personal-crm-app-clay-introduces-an-ai-helper-to-help-you-navigate-your-relationships)). **Reconnect** cadences are Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly, *Automatic* (suggested from your last interaction) or Disable, and can be set per person or per group. The clock counts from the most recent interaction it sees (calls, calendar, email, messages), so reaching out early pushes the next nudge back ([Mesh help: Reconnect](https://library.me.sh/hc/en-us/articles/6817306147355)). Don't confuse it with clay.com, the separate GTM enrichment company. Write-ups about *that* Clay warn the AI "can misidentify job titles" and tell you to review updates before acting on them ([Asian Efficiency on clay.com](https://www.asianefficiency.com/technology/build-ai-personal-crm-clay/)).

**Dex.** It syncs LinkedIn (bio, work history, title-change alerts), Gmail, Google Calendar and Outlook, syncs contacts both ways with the device address book, has AI-assigned tags, and offers keep-in-touch from one month to one year plus birthday reminders ([Dex core docs](https://getdex.com/docs/dex-core)). There are two kinds of reminder. **Standard** reminders fire on a set date. **Keep-in-touch (KIT)** reminders are "calculated from last interaction + frequency" and move whenever an interaction is logged ([Dex reminders](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/reminders)). With no last interaction on record, the clock starts today ([Dex keep-in-touch](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/keep-in-touch)).

**folk.** It has four concepts: records (people, companies, deals), fields, groups and views. **Native** fields and **smart** (computed) fields such as "My last interaction" are shared across the workspace. **Custom** fields "belong to a single group," so deleting a group deletes those fields but keeps the people ([folk data model](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/9790806-folk-data-model)). Its reminders are plain dated reminders that can repeat weekly, monthly or yearly, and they are *not* tied to interactions: "reminders are only based on the date you set" ([folk reminders](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/5658439-set-reminders-on-your-contacts)).

**Covve** sends "one short weekly list of people to get in touch with," with a custom interval per person, private notes, tags by relationship type, and business-card scanning ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/covve-intelligent-address-book/id958935377)). **UpHabit** has regular or custom reminders, snoozing during busy periods, searchable notes, tags, introduction templates and contact sync ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1335632832)).

**Obsidian people notes.** The recurring tension is that YAML front matter is "great for queries" but "messy" for nuanced history, so people mix front matter for identity fields with inline `Field:: [[Link]]` fields or prose in the body ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/structuring-crm-data-in-a-note-front-matter-vs-note-content/17128)). A popular setup keeps a People MOC (an index note) that lists people notes and links them to meeting notes, so interactions live in the meeting notes and not on the person ([dannberg gist](https://gist.github.com/dannberg/8e021e84f6c5024dcdfe89de909e3335)).

### Standards and platform contact models

**vCard 4.0 (RFC 6350).** It defines FN, N, NICKNAME, BDAY, ANNIVERSARY, GENDER, TEL, EMAIL, IMPP, TZ, GEO, ORG, TITLE, ROLE, MEMBER, **RELATED**, CATEGORIES, NOTE, REV and UID. RELATED takes a TYPE from a fixed vocabulary that includes friend, acquaintance, met, colleague, co-worker, neighbor, child, parent, sibling, spouse, kin, crush and emergency. KIND can be individual, group, org or location. For sync, **UID** identifies a card and **PID + CLIENTPIDMAP** identify each property instance by source, so two clients can merge the same phone number without duplicating it ([RFC 6350](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350.txt)).

**Google People API.** The Person resource has `names`, `nicknames`, `biographies`, `birthdays`, `events`, `interests`, `relations`, `memberships`, `userDefined`, `clientData`, and more. Every field item carries `metadata.source` (where it came from) and `primary`/`sourcePrimary` flags, because one Person is a *merge* of several sources. `metadata.sources`, `linkedPeopleResourceNames` and `previousResourceNames` record that merge history ([People API reference](https://developers.google.com/people/api/rest/v1/people)). This is the cleanest public example of **per-field provenance**.

### Business CRMs

**HubSpot.** Core objects are Contacts, Companies, Deals and Tickets. Calls, emails, meetings, notes and tasks are separate **activity** objects. **Associations** link records and can carry labels such as "Decision Maker." Email is the dedup key for contacts and domain for companies ([HubSpot: understanding the CRM](https://developers.hubspot.com/docs/api/crm/understanding-the-crm)). There are three "recency" properties, and they mean different things. **Last contacted** covers logged chats, calls, sales emails and meetings, but *not* notes or tasks. **Last activity date** adds notes and tasks. **Last engagement date** tracks actions the *other* person takes, such as opens, clicks and bookings ([HubSpot community thread](https://community.hubspot.com/t/last-activity-date-vs-last-contacted-whats-the-difference/35753)). Merging keeps the primary record's values, fills its blanks from the secondary, keeps the "furthest-along" lifecycle stage, turns the secondary email into a secondary address, combines both timelines, and gives the result a new ID. It **cannot be undone** ([HubSpot merge docs](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/merge-records)).

**Salesforce.** A **Lead** is an unqualified prospect. Converting it creates an **Account** (the company), a **Contact** (the person) and optionally an **Opportunity** (the deal, with stage, amount, close date and probability) ([Trailhead](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/leads_opportunities_lightning_experience/create-and-convert-leads-lightning)). Activities are **Events** (time-bound) or **Tasks** (to-dos, plus call and email logs). Two polymorphic links attach them: **WhoId** points at a person (lead or contact) and **WhatId** points at a thing (account, opportunity). The UI splits them into **Open Activities** (future) and **Activity History** (past) ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-activities-everything-you-need-to-know/)).

**Where auto-logging and enrichment go wrong:** "last contacted" depends on which channels are connected, and HubSpot alone counts three different ways. Metadata-only ingestion misses everything that happened offline. Enrichment can attach the wrong job title or the wrong person. Merges are destructive.

## 2. Relationship science behind cadence

- **Tie strength.** Granovetter defines it as "a (probably linear) combination of the amount of time, the emotional intensity, the intimacy … and the reciprocal services." Because "all bridges are weak ties," weak ties carry new information across groups ([Granovetter 1973](https://smg.media.mit.edu/classes/library/granovetter.weak.ties/granovetter.html)).
- **Weak ties, tested causally.** A randomized experiment on about 20 million LinkedIn users from 2015 to 2019 found that **moderately** weak ties produced the most job moves. On mutual connections the curve is an inverted U peaking near 10 mutuals. On interaction intensity the weakest ties did best. The effect was stronger in digital industries ([MIT News on Rajkumar et al. 2022, Science](https://news.mit.edu/2022/weak-ties-linkedin-employment-0915)). Implication: don't let cadence logic bury acquaintances.
- **Network size and layers.** Christmas-card networks averaged about 153, with inflection points near 7, 20 and 35. The closest contacts were reached about weekly, and time since last contact fell as emotional closeness rose ([Hill & Dunbar 2003](https://bebr.ufl.edu/sites/default/files/Hill%20and%20Dunbar%20-%202003%20-%20Social%20network%20size%20in%20humans.pdf)). Layers are usually cited as 5/15/50/150 with a scaling ratio of about 3. In phone-call data only the outer two layers were well supported ([MacCarron, Kaski & Dunbar](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02400v1)). About 40% of social effort goes to the closest 5 and about 60% to the closest 15 ([Dunbar 2020, Proc R Soc A](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f56e97cb-c481-449c-b792-a76156550d9e/files/swm117p24t)).
- **Decay without contact.** Over 18 months, friendships lost more emotional closeness than kin relationships when contact frequency and shared activities dropped. Friendships cost more to maintain ([Roberts & Dunbar 2011](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:0a8fe8a0-ee1a-47de-b70f-06da958af4a5)). Dunbar later summarizes that family ties are "quite robust" while friendships fade "within a matter of a few months" without contact ([Dunbar 2020](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f56e97cb-c481-449c-b792-a76156550d9e/files/swm117p24t)). Higher closeness also means shorter time since last contact, more so for kin ([Roberts & Dunbar, Personal Relationships](https://www.neuroscience.ox.ac.uk/publications/304688)).
- **Critique.** Re-running Dunbar's primate analysis with modern methods gave 95% confidence intervals of roughly **2 to 520**. A precise human estimate "is not possible" ([Stockholm University on Lindenfors et al. 2021](https://www.su.se/english/news/articles/2021-05-05-new-study-deconstructs-dunbars-number---yes-you-can-have-more-than-150-friends), a press release). Treat the layers as a useful shape, not as hard caps.
- **Building ties.** It takes roughly 50 hours to go from acquaintance to casual friend, about 90 hours to friend, and over 200 hours to close friend. Casual social time counts; time working together counts much less ([KU on Hall 2019](https://news.ku.edu/2018/03/06/study-reveals-number-hours-it-takes-make-friend)).
- **Can strength be computed?** A Facebook model predicted tie strength with 87% accuracy. **Days since last communication** (β −0.76) and **days since first communication** (β +0.755) were its strongest predictors. It failed when the relationship lived offline: one close friend scored 0.47 against a true 0.96 ([Gilbert & Karahalios 2009](https://collablab.northwestern.edu/CollabolabDistro/nucmc/GilbertKarahalios-PredictingTieStrength-CHI09.pdf)). Affinity's commercial score likewise "analyzes the email and calendar interactions" ([Affinity](https://support.affinity.co/s/article/Relationship-intelligence)), so it is blind in the same way.

**Cadence defaults in practice:** Mesh offers weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly or Automatic. Dex offers one month to one year. Covve batches everything into one weekly list. None of them publishes a research-backed default.

## 3. Organization patterns

- **Append-only log plus current view (event sourcing).** Store "a sequence of events" as the source of truth, rebuild state by replaying them, answer "what was true at time T" with temporal queries, and use snapshots as a cache ([Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)). For people this means the chat log and extracted facts are the events, and the card is a snapshot you can regenerate.
- **Bitemporal facts (Graphiti/Zep).** Each edge carries `created_at`/`expired_at` (when the system believed it) and `valid_at`/`invalid_at` (when it was true in the world). A contradicting fact *expires* the old edge and does not delete it, and the expired fact gets rewritten in past tense ("used to"). Out-of-order episodes are resolved by `valid_at` ([Zep blog](https://www.getzep.com/blog/beyond-static-knowledge-graphs/)). The pipeline runs: extract entities → extract facts → date them → resolve entities, preferring "**under-merge** over **over-merge**" → resolve facts → refresh each entity's summary ([Zep: how graph creation works](https://help.getzep.com/how-graph-creation-works)). The last step *is* the "interaction log + summary card" pattern.
- **Entity-attribute-value with provenance.** Google's per-field `source` and `primary` flags, and vCard's PID per property instance, show the same idea: store each fact with where it came from, and pick one primary value for display ([People API](https://developers.google.com/people/api/rest/v1/people), [RFC 6350](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350.txt)).
- **Identity resolution and merges.** HubSpot dedups on email and its merges are irreversible ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/merge-records)). Google keeps `previousResourceNames` and linked people ([People API](https://developers.google.com/people/api/rest/v1/people)). Zep would rather leave two nodes than collapse two people into one ([Zep](https://help.getzep.com/how-graph-creation-works)). In a chat-only setting there is no email key, only names, nicknames and context ("my buddy from work"), so under-merge plus an alias list plus a reversible merge is the safe combination.
- **Open loops / next step.** Salesforce's split between Open Activities and Activity History ([Salesforce Ben](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-activities-everything-you-need-to-know/)) and Dex's standard vs KIT reminders ([Dex](https://getdex.com/docs/workflows/reminders)) both separate *things owed* from *things that happened*.
- **Tags vs hierarchy.** folk scopes custom fields to groups, so a person can sit in many contexts ([folk](https://help.folk.app/en/articles/9790806-folk-data-model)). vCard uses flat CATEGORIES ([RFC 6350](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350.txt)). PARA sorts by actionability: Projects have deadlines, Areas are "a sphere of activity with a standard to be maintained over time," Resources are topics of interest, and Archives are inactive ([Forte](https://every.to/praxis/para-an-introduction-827085)). A person is an Area and an interest is a Resource, so tags beat folders.

## Implications for Long Con people cards

- **The log is the truth and the card is a cache.** Keep dated facts append-only with a pointer to their source turn, and regenerate the card from them the way Zep refreshes entity summaries ([Zep](https://help.getzep.com/how-graph-creation-works), [Fowler](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html)). Never edit a card in place without logging a fact.
- **Supersede facts; don't delete them.** Give each fact `observed_at` (the turn) and an optional `valid_from`/`valid_to`, so "Sam changed teams" expires "Sam is on team A" and the old fact stays as history ([Graphiti](https://www.getzep.com/blog/beyond-static-knowledge-graphs/)).
- **Every fact needs provenance:** a source (user said / inferred / imported) plus the turn id, like Google's per-field `source` ([People API](https://developers.google.com/people/api/rest/v1/people)). This keeps inferred facts out of the packet when confidence matters.
- **Under-merge identities.** Keep an `aliases` list and record merges as a reversible event (a `merged_into` field), not a destructive rewrite. HubSpot's irreversible merges are the cautionary tale ([HubSpot](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/records/merge-records), [Zep](https://help.getzep.com/how-graph-creation-works)).
- **Define "last contact" precisely.** Separate `last_mentioned` (the user talked *about* them) from `last_interaction` (the user reports actually talking *with* them). HubSpot needs three fields because these differ ([HubSpot thread](https://community.hubspot.com/t/last-activity-date-vs-last-contacted-whats-the-difference/35753)).
- **Make cadence optional and relative**, Dex-KIT style: `next_due = last_interaction + cadence`, with no default. The evidence supports *decay* for friends and not for kin ([Roberts & Dunbar 2011](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:0a8fe8a0-ee1a-47de-b70f-06da958af4a5)) but not exact numbers ([Lindenfors](https://www.su.se/english/news/articles/2021-05-05-new-study-deconstructs-dunbars-number---yes-you-can-have-more-than-150-friends)). A `kin` flag is worth more than a Dunbar layer.
- **Don't compute a strength score from chat mentions.** The best predictor, recency of communication, is invisible when the relationship lives offline, and that is exactly where Gilbert's model broke ([Gilbert & Karahalios](https://collablab.northwestern.edu/CollabolabDistro/nucmc/GilbertKarahalios-PredictingTieStrength-CHI09.pdf)). Use a coarse user-stated `closeness` instead.
- **Keep open loops separate from history.** Promises, gift ideas, "ask about X next time" and money owed go in an `open_loops` list with a status. Those are the items that earn a place in the packet ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-activities-everything-you-need-to-know/), [Monica features](https://monicahq.com/features)).
- **Use a small typed vocabulary for relationships**, borrowed from vCard RELATED (spouse, child, friend, colleague, kin, met …), plus free text for the specifics ([RFC 6350](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350.txt)).
- **Interests are dated notes, not tags.** An interest has a first-seen and last-seen turn and can go stale. Treat it as a PARA Resource linked to people, not a field on the card ([Forte](https://every.to/praxis/para-an-introduction-827085)).
- **Keep weak ties.** Moderately weak ties carry the most new opportunities ([Rajkumar et al. via MIT](https://news.mit.edu/2022/weak-ties-linkedin-employment-0915)), so the card store should hold acquaintances cheaply, and packet selection, not storage, should do the pruning.

**Suggested minimal schema** (sketch only; nothing is being built yet):

```yaml
person:
  id: p_sam                 # stable, never reused
  name: Sam
  aliases: [Sam, "Sammy", "my buddy from work"]
  relation: [colleague, friend]   # vCard RELATED vocab
  kin: false
  closeness: close            # user-stated: inner|close|regular|acquaintance
  cadence_days: null          # optional, KIT-style
  last_interaction: 2026-11-21   # derived from facts
  last_mentioned: 2026-11-25     # derived from facts
  summary: "..."              # regenerated cache, never hand-edited
  merged_into: null
fact:                          # append-only
  id: f_123
  person: p_sam
  text: "just started learning pottery"
  kind: attribute|event|preference|open_loop|interest
  source: stated|inferred|imported
  turn: t_8812                 # provenance
  observed_at: 2026-11-21
  valid_from: null
  valid_to: null               # set when superseded
  superseded_by: null
  status: open|done            # open_loop only
```

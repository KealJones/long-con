# Sales: cold outreach, engagement, relationships

Researched 2026-11-26. Every claim links to a page that was actually read. **Evidence key:**
- **Experiment**: peer-reviewed, randomized or controlled.
- **Peer-reviewed observational**: published, but correlational or field data.
- **Vendor data**: self-published by a company that sells the tool or the training. Large samples, but no peer review, no published method, success often undefined, and the vendor has a reason to want an interesting result.
- **Practitioner opinion**: books, frameworks, anecdote.

The honest headline: almost all the famous sales stats are vendor data. Most of the sound science is about **liking, reaching out and remembering people**, not about scripts.

---

## 1. Cold calls

**Gong openers.** The 2018 Gong study of 90,380 first-touch cold calls reported a 1.5% baseline success rate. "How have you been?" scored 10.01%, or 6.6x. "The reason for my call is…" scored 2.1x. "Did I catch you at a bad time?" scored 0.9%, about 40% *below* baseline ([Gong, cold call opening lines](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines)). The post never defines "success" beyond booking a meeting, and it gives no significance tests or coding method.

A later Gong post covers "300M+ calls." It ranks "Heard the name tossed around?" first at 11.24% and a **permission-based opener** ("own that it's a cold call, then ask permission to pitch") a close second at 11.18%. "Did I catch you at a bad time?" comes last at 2.15%, which the post calls "only slightly above the average baseline" ([Gong, best and worst openers](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls)). That contradicts the earlier "40% below baseline" figure, and neither post explains the change. Treat the direction as plausible and the multipliers as marketing.

Note the tension: Chris Voss recommends "Is now a bad time to talk?" as a no-oriented question ([Science of People summary](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/never-split-difference-summary/)), while Gong's data says that line performs worst. Neither side has a controlled test.

**Call shape (Gong).** Successful cold calls run about twice as long. Reps talk about 55% of the time, the average monologue is 53 s versus 25 s, and there is 65% more "we" language. The sample is described only as "thousands of cold calls" ([Gong, cold call stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats)). Longer calls are likely an effect of success, not a cause: people who are interested stay on the line.

**Talk-to-listen.** The famous 43:57 "golden ratio" comes from a 2016 Gong study. Gong's newer analysis of 326K sales calls (each over 10 minutes) finds won deals average 57% rep talk and lost deals 62%. Top performers' talk share stays roughly constant whether they win or lose ([Gong, talk-to-listen](https://www.gong.io/blog/talk-to-listen-conversion-ratio)). Again correlational: a prospect who is talking a lot is often already engaged.

**Speed to lead (the strongest of the "sales stats").** A 2007 MIT/InsideSales study drew on 6 companies, 15,000+ web leads and 100,000+ dials over 3 years. It found the odds of *contacting* a lead fall about 100x, and the odds of qualifying it about 21x, if the first call comes at 30 minutes instead of 5. Wednesday and Thursday were best and 4–6 pm best for contact. The authors flag that the source is a vendor co-authored presentation, it reports only odds ratios, and "qualified" was defined differently at each company ([Lead Response Management study PDF](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)).

The HBR follow-up (Oldroyd, McElheran & Elkington, 2011) audited 2,241 US firms. Only 37% responded within an hour, 23% never responded, and the average was 42 hours. A separate dataset of 1.25M leads showed firms calling within an hour were about 7x likelier to qualify a lead than an hour later, and 60x likelier than after 24 hours ([SmartCompany summary of the HBR study](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/); [BYU record](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711)). This is about **inbound** leads, not cold calls, and it is observational: firms that answer fast may also be better at everything else. The *direction* (respond while interest is warm) is very likely right.

**Best times / connect rates.** Orum claims an analysis of over 1 billion dials with time-of-day heatmaps by role, but the public page gives no numbers ([Orum, State of Cold Calling](https://www.orum.com/reports-and-guides/the-state-of-cold-calling)). "Best time to call" claims mostly trace back to the 2007 study above. Treat them as weak.

**RAIN Group (buyer survey).** RAIN surveyed 488 buyers and 489 sellers. 82% of buyers say they accept meetings with sellers who proactively reach out, 71% want to talk early while exploring ideas, and 52% of sellers say it takes 5–10 touches to connect. Buyers are swayed by research relevant to their business (69%) and by content customized to their situation (67%) ([CustomerThink release](https://customerthink.com/new-rain-group-center-for-sales-research-study-debunks-common-myths-about-sales-prospecting/); [RAIN research page](https://www.rainsalestraining.com/sales-research/sales-prospecting-research)). Self-reported survey data from a training vendor: "82% accept meetings" is what buyers *say*.

## 2. Cold email and LinkedIn

| Source (all vendor data) | Finding |
|---|---|
| [Backlinko × Pitchbox, 12M emails](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study) | 8.5% reply rate overall. One follow-up raised replies 65.8%. Personalized body +32.7%, personalized subject +30.5%. Emailing several contacts at one org +93%. **Caveat: SEO and link-building outreach, not sales.** |
| [Gong, cold email (28M+)](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/does-cold-email-even-work-any-more-heres-what-the-data-says/) | 100 words or fewer, 3–4 sentences. Pitching in the body cuts replies up to 57%. The average rep needs about 344 emails per meeting. |
| [Gong, CTA study (304K emails)](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings) | In *cold* emails an **interest CTA** ("Interested in learning more?") beat asking for a specific time. Once a deal is in progress, a specific time ask wins (37% vs 25%). Success = meeting within 10 days. |
| [Instantly 2026 benchmark](https://instantly.ai/report-page) | Average reply rate 3.43%, top 10% 10.7%+. The first email gets 58% of replies and follow-ups 42%. 4–7 touches, 3–4 days apart. The best senders keep first emails under 80 words. |
| [Woodpecker follow-ups](https://woodpecker.co/blog/follow-up/follow-up-statistics/) | About 9% replies without a follow-up vs 13% with one. 2–3 follow-ups is the sweet spot. The first follow-up adds the most. Data sources unstated. |
| [Hunter 2025 (11M emails + 217-person survey)](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025) | Average reply rate 4.1%. Campaigns to 50 or fewer recipients get 5.8%, to 1,000+ only 2.1%. With follow-ups 4.9%, without 3%. **Unsubscribes rise after the 3rd follow-up.** |
| [Lavender benchmark (231K emails)](https://lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report) | Reply rates vary by persona (technical buyers 5.2%). "A-grade" emails lift replies up to 79% for finance. The method is their own scoring. |

**What the email data agrees on:** short (under about 100 words); about the recipient's problem, not your pitch; a low-commitment ask on first touch; 1–3 follow-ups, with the first one doing most of the work; small, targeted lists beat blasts. **What is marketing:** precise lift percentages, "best day to send," and every claim that the vendor's own feature is the key variable. No study here randomizes anything.

## 3. Methodologies and their evidence

- **SPIN Selling (Rackham).** Based on 35,000 observed calls in 20+ countries over 12 years, with a 30-person team ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Rackham)). Observers had to reach a 0.85 agreement with standard tapes. Rackham credits the gains (for example a 17% volume lift against control groups) to the whole coaching process, not to the questions alone ([Huthwaite](https://www.huthwaiteinternational.com/blog/neil-rackham-research-spin)). Core findings: closing techniques help small sales but hurt large ones. Big deals need *implied* needs (problems) turned into *explicit* needs (wanting a fix) through Implication and Need-payoff questions. Offering solutions too early creates objections ([SuperSummary](https://supersummary.com/spin-selling/summary)). The best evidence base of any method, but it was never peer-reviewed and is now dated.
- **The Challenger Sale (CEB).** Five rep profiles, and "nearly 40%" of high performers were Challengers ([Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/de/ceb-research-challenger-sale/)). Noel Capon (Columbia) calls the book a "breakthrough built on a flimsy foundation" and an infomercial for its sponsor, CEB ([Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/revisiting-challenger-sale-breakthrough-built-flimsy-foundation)). Sponsor-run and unreplicated.
- **Sandler** (1966): an up-front contract on time and outcomes, a pain funnel of open questions, and qualifying budget and commitment early. The source cites no outcome data ([Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/sandler-sales-methodology/)).
- **MEDDIC** (PTC, 1996): Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identify pain, Champion. Built from a review of won, lost and slipped deals. The "$0 to $1B" growth claim comes from the certification seller and is unsourced ([MEDDICC](https://meddicc.com/resources/who-created-meddic)).
- **Gap Selling** (Keenan, 2018): map the current state, the future state and the gap between them, and quantify the impact. No outcome data ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/gap-selling)).
- **Never Split the Difference (Voss).** Mirroring means repeating the last 1–3 words. Labeling means "It sounds like…". It also uses calibrated how and what questions, aims for "That's right," and favors no-oriented questions. The summary cites no research, only FBI experience ([Science of People](https://www.scienceofpeople.com/never-split-difference-summary/)). **Adjacent science does exist:**
  - *Labeling:* naming an emotion reduced amygdala activity in an fMRI study of 30 adults. The lead author calls the effect "likely modest" ([UCLA Health](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/release/putting-feelings-into-words-produces-therapeutic-effects-in-the-brain-ucla-neuroimaging-study-supports-ancient-buddhist-teachings)). That is a lab face-labeling task, not negotiation.
  - *Verbal mimicry and tips:* a waitress who repeated orders back verbatim got significantly larger tips, in two experiments ([van Baaren et al. 2003](https://stafforini.com/works/van-baaren-2003-mimicry-money-behavioral/)).
  - *Mimicry in negotiation:* when buyers mimicked, 67% of dyads reached a deal, versus 12.5% without mimicry (N = 31 dyads, MBA students), and the effect was mediated by trust ([Maddux, Mullen & Galinsky 2008](https://willmaddux.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/15846/2018/04/JESP-Mimicking-and-Negotiations.pdf)).
  - *Language style matching:* in 9 real hostage crises, successful negotiations had much higher conversational LSM (r = .46 vs .05). The authors call this tentative because the sample is small ([Taylor & Thomas](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/26912/1/1.pdf)).
  - *Mimicry overall:* a meta-analysis calls coordination effects "robust" ([Vicaria & Dickens 2016](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10919-016-0238-8)). But a 2023 preregistered set of studies found **noticeable mimicry backfires**, and in the online study even "subtle" mimicry was noticed and hurt ([J Nonverbal Behav 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10919-023-00446-5)).

## 4. Relationships and account management

- **Asking follow-up questions makes you more liked.** In 600+ online chatters and 110 speed-daters (2,000+ dates), people who asked more questions, especially follow-ups, were better liked. One more question per date was worth about one more "yes" across 20 dates. The caveat: a barrage of questions without self-disclosure feels invasive ([HBS Working Knowledge on Huang et al. 2017](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/asking-questions-can-get-you-a-better-job-or-a-second-date)).
- **Escalating self-disclosure builds closeness (Aron et al., 36 questions).** 190 stranger pairs who took turns on increasingly personal questions felt closer than pairs on superficial questions. The effect held even when partners disagreed or were told closeness was the goal. The authors say the result does not create loyalty or commitment ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/fast-tracking-intimacy)).
- **The liking gap.** Across 5 studies, after a conversation people underestimate how much the other person liked them. The gap persisted across conversation lengths and repeated conversations ([Yale News on Boothby et al. 2018](https://news.yale.edu/2018/09/04/first-meeting-people-you-more-you-think)).
- **Reaching out is appreciated more than senders expect.** In studies with 5,900+ participants, recipients of a "just checking in" note valued it more than senders predicted. The gap was largest when the contact was surprising or the tie was weak ([APA on Liu et al. 2022](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached)).
- **Gratitude letters.** Across 4 experiments, writers underestimated how positive recipients would feel and overestimated how awkward they would feel. Recipients cared about warmth, not wording ([UChicago on Kumar & Epley 2018](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/people-underestimate-value-sending-letters-appreciation)).
- **Cialdini's liking principle:** we say yes to people we like, through similarity, compliments, cooperation and familiarity ([ChangingMinds](https://www.changingminds.org/techniques/general/cialdini/liking.htm)). On compliments: even flattery with an obvious ulterior motive leaves a positive *implicit* attitude that drives behavior after people consciously discount it ([AMA on Chan & Sengupta 2010](https://www.ama.org/research-insights/research-insight-even-phony-flattery-helps-close-deals/)).
- **Relationship marketing meta-analysis** (94 studies, about 38,000 relationships): relationship quality predicts performance best, and commitment worst. Relationships with an **individual person** are more effective than relationships with the selling firm ([AMA on Palmatier et al. 2006](https://ama.org/2019/01/08/factors-influencing-the-effectiveness-of-relationship-marketing-a-meta-analysis)). This is the best evidence that remembering a *person* matters.
- **Key accounts are not automatically profitable.** At one consulting firm (295 customers), key accounts started with the best margins but fell below small and large accounts by year 3. They did survive at the highest rate. Possible causes include discount pressure, cost to serve, and complacency ([Sharma & Evanschitzky](https://publications.aston.ac.uk/id/eprint/28014/1/Returns_on_key_accounts.pdf)).
- **Retention economics.** "Retaining 5% more customers can almost double profits" comes from Reichheld & Sasser 1990. The accessible version leans on an MBNA case study with no stated method ([Bain/HBR](https://www.bain.com/el/insights/zero-defections-quality-comes-to-services-harvard-business-review-hbr/)). The direction is widely accepted, the exact numbers are not.
- **What top account managers track.** The Mackay 66 is a 66-question profile of the *person* (character, accomplishments, life outside work, self-image, what makes them tick). Mackay says it should be guarded closely and not treated as gossip ([Inc.](https://www.inc.com/harvey-mackay/66-ways-to-know-your-customer-your-real-customer.html)). Practitioner opinion.
- **When remembering backfires.** Personalized ads raised click-through when data was collected *overtly*, but the effect reversed when collection was *covert*, because people felt vulnerable. Trust signals offset it ([Aguirre et al. 2015](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jouret:v:91:y:2015:i:1:p:34-49)). Showing why someone sees an ad hurts when it reveals cross-site tracking or *inferred* traits, and helps when the data was collected on the same site ([BU on Kim, Barasz & John](https://www.bu.edu/susilo/2020/05/30/can-online-ad-transparency-enhance-ad-effectiveness)).

## Tactics worth knowing (ranked by evidence)

1. Ask follow-up questions, balanced with your own disclosure ([HBS](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/asking-questions-can-get-you-a-better-job-or-a-second-date)).
2. Reach out unprompted and say specific thanks. People undervalue both, and recipients like them more than you'd guess ([APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached), [UChicago](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/people-underestimate-value-sending-letters-appreciation)).
3. Assume they liked you more than you think, and follow up anyway ([Yale](https://news.yale.edu/2018/09/04/first-meeting-people-you-more-you-think)).
4. Build the relationship with the person, and invest in relationship quality ([AMA meta-analysis](https://ama.org/2019/01/08/factors-influencing-the-effectiveness-of-relationship-marketing-a-meta-analysis)).
5. Gradual, reciprocal self-disclosure ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/fast-tracking-intimacy)).
6. Repeat their key words back, subtly and never obviously ([Maddux](https://willmaddux.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/15846/2018/04/JESP-Mimicking-and-Negotiations.pdf), [JNB 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10919-023-00446-5)).
7. Be transparent about what you remember, and use what they told you, not what you inferred ([BU](https://www.bu.edu/susilo/2020/05/30/can-online-ad-transparency-enhance-ad-effectiveness)).
8. Respond to inbound interest fast ([MIT/InsideSales](https://www.mortech.com/hs-fs/hub/25649/file-13535879-pdf/docs/mit_study.pdf)).
9. Explore the problem's consequences before pitching, and propose a next step instead of a hard close ([SuperSummary](https://supersummary.com/spin-selling/summary)).
10. Cold email: short, about them, an interest-style ask, 1–3 follow-ups (vendor consensus, see section 2).
11. Cold call: say who you are and why you're calling, ask permission, listen (vendor data only).

## Implications for what an assistant should remember about contacts and follow-ups

- **Store what the person said, in their own words, with the date and source.** Follow-up questions and verbatim echoes are the best-supported rapport tools, and both need the exact phrasing ([HBS](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/asking-questions-can-get-you-a-better-job-or-a-second-date), [van Baaren](https://stafforini.com/works/van-baaren-2003-mimicry-money-behavioral/)).
- **Keep "open threads" per contact.** Things they mentioned that invite a later follow-up question ("my kid starts school Monday," "we're migrating off X in Q2"), each with a date to resurface it.
- **Separate stated facts from inferences, and never surface an inference as if it were known.** Inferred traits and data the person didn't expect you to have are where personalization turns creepy ([Aguirre](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jouret:v:91:y:2015:i:1:p:34-49), [BU](https://www.bu.edu/susilo/2020/05/30/can-online-ad-transparency-enhance-ad-effectiveness)).
- **Track provenance** (told me directly / public profile / third party). Only "told me directly" is safe to mention casually.
- **Track the last contact date and nudge "reach out" for weak or dormant ties.** Surprise check-ins are underrated, most of all for weak ties ([APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached)).
- **Log things to thank people for** (intros, help, favors) and prompt a specific thank-you. Recipients care about warmth, not polish ([UChicago](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/people-underestimate-value-sending-letters-appreciation)).
- **Record the person, not just the company:** role, what they're proud of, what they care about outside work (Mackay-style), since relationships with individuals drive results ([AMA](https://ama.org/2019/01/08/factors-influencing-the-effectiveness-of-relationship-marketing-a-meta-analysis), [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/harvey-mackay/66-ways-to-know-your-customer-your-real-customer.html)). Guard it, and let the user mark fields private.
- **Note the reciprocity balance**: what they shared versus what the user shared, so the assistant can suggest some self-disclosure instead of more questions ([APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/fast-tracking-intimacy)).
- **Store the need in SPIN/Gap terms for deals:** current problem, its consequences, desired future state, decision process and champion (MEDDIC fields). Useful structure, but practitioner-grade evidence.
- **Store the follow-up cadence and stop rules:** touches so far, channel, and a stop after about 3 unanswered follow-ups, since unsubscribes rise after that ([Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)).
- **Time-sensitive inbound:** flag new inbound interest for a same-hour response ([HBR summary](https://www.smartcompany.com.au/marketing/20110704-are-you-letting-online-leads-go-cold/)).
- **Counter the liking gap:** after a first meeting, default to suggesting a follow-up rather than waiting for "a sign" ([Yale](https://news.yale.edu/2018/09/04/first-meeting-people-you-more-you-think)).
